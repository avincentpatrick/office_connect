# agent8
TOPIC: Philippine government rules for local travel reimbursement that Office-Connect's Local Travel Reimbursement module must encode: EO 77 s. 2019 rate/computation rules, COA documentary requirements (Circulars 2012-001, 2023-004, 97-002/96-004), GAM for NGAs paper forms (Appendices 44-47), liquidation deadlines, and DOH approval-chain baseline (DO 2019-0225)

## KEY PRACTICES
- PRACTICE: Base the rate engine on EO 77 s. 2019 (signed 2019-03-15), which superseded EO 248/248-A/298. Local travel uses a Daily Travel Expense (DTE) set by DESTINATION cluster in Annex A: Cluster I PHP 1,500 (Regions I, II, III, V, VIII, IX, XII, XIII/Caraga, BARMM), Cluster II PHP 1,800 (CAR, Regions VI, VII, X, XI), Cluster III PHP 2,200 (NCR, Regions IV-A, IV-B). No rate-amending EO or circular was found as of mid-2026, but rates must be stored as effective-dated config, not constants.
  WHY: Every peso computed by the module traces to this EO; COA disallows amounts above the Annex A rate absent the excess-claim certification path. Cluster is keyed to where the employee travels TO, so a multi-leg trip can have different rates per day.
  SOURCE: https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2019/03/15/executive-order-no-77-s-2019/
- PRACTICE: Apportion the DTE 50% hotel/lodging, 30% meals, 20% incidental expenses, and compute per-day entitlement from the itinerary: 100% of DTE on the day of arrival at the destination (regardless of arrival time) and on each succeeding full day of official business; only 50% (30% meals + 20% incidentals, no lodging component) on the day of departure back to the permanent official station.
  WHY: This is the core computation COA auditors re-perform on every claim; getting the return-day percentage wrong is a classic disallowance. The lodging component exists only for nights actually spent at the destination.
  SOURCE: https://gabotaf.com/eo-77-a-quick-comprehensive-guide-to-the-grant-of-daily-travel-expenses-dte/
- PRACTICE: Enforce the 50-kilometer rule: full DTE (including lodging) applies to travel beyond a 50-km radius from the permanent official station. Within 50 km, DTE and lodging may be claimed ONLY if the employee actually stays overnight at the place of assignment and does not commute daily between residence/station and the assignment; a daily commuter within 50 km gets transportation fare only.
  WHY: This is EO 77's primary anti-abuse control and a standing COA audit test; the system needs official-station and destination data (plus an overnight-stay attestation) to gate the lodging and DTE components.
  SOURCE: https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2019/03/15/executive-order-no-77-s-2019/
- PRACTICE: Receipts policy: the lodging component is payable only against actual hotel/lodging bills and official receipts; meals and incidentals within the DTE need no receipts. Claims in excess of the prescribed DTE are allowed only with (a) certification by the agency head that the excess was absolutely necessary and (b) supporting bills/receipts, with the excess capped (commonly stated as not more than 100% of the component). A certification or affidavit of loss is explicitly NOT an acceptable substitute for hotel/lodging bills and receipts.
  WHY: COA has repeatedly disallowed lodging paid without receipts or supported only by affidavits of loss; the app must hard-block those states rather than warn.
  SOURCE: https://www.coa.gov.ph/wpfd_file/coa-circular-no-2012-001-june-14-2012/
- PRACTICE: Encode the COA documentary checklists (COA Circular 2012-001 sec. 1.2.4.1, updated by COA Circular 2023-004 of 2023-06-14). Grant of cash advance for local travel: (1) duly approved Office Order/Travel Order, (2) duly approved Itinerary of Travel, (3) certification from the Chief Accountant that previous cash advances have been liquidated and accounted for. Liquidation/reimbursement: paper/electronic tickets (plane/boat/bus), boarding pass, terminal fee receipts, Certificate of Appearance/Attendance from the office visited, copy of the approved Itinerary of Travel plus a Revised Itinerary if the actual travel deviated, Certificate of Travel Completed, Liquidation Report, Reimbursement Expense Receipt (RER) for fares with no receipts (jeepney/tricycle/etc.), hotel/lodging bills with official receipts when overnight stay is claimed, agency-head certification for expenses in excess of prescribed rates, and the official receipt for any refund of excess cash advance.
  WHY: These checklists ARE the audit compliance surface: a claim that reaches Accounting without a required document is returned or disallowed, so the app should compute the conditional checklist from claim facts and block submission until mandatory items are attached.
  SOURCE: https://www.coa.gov.ph/wpfd_file/coa-circular-no-2023-004-june-14-2023/
- PRACTICE: Mirror the official GAM for NGAs Volume II forms with their correct appendix numbers: Appendix 32 Disbursement Voucher, Appendix 44 Liquidation Report (LR), Appendix 45 Itinerary of Travel (IoT), Appendix 46 Reimbursement Expense Receipt (RER), Appendix 47 Certificate of Travel Completed (CTC). The IoT captures per-leg date, places, departure/arrival times, means of transportation, fare per leg, per diem per day, and totals, certified by the traveler and approved by the supervisor. (Note: the widely remembered 'Appendix 44 = Itinerary of Travel' is the OLD pre-2015 NGAS numbering; under GAM 2015 the IoT is Appendix 45.)
  WHY: Accounting staff and COA auditors expect printouts that match these exact forms; generating pixel-faithful App 44/45/46/47 PDFs from structured data is what makes a digital claim acceptable in a paper-based post-audit.
  SOURCE: https://www.treasury.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Table-of-Contents-Volume-II.pdf
- PRACTICE: Enforce cash-advance discipline per COA Circular 97-002 (and 96-004): liquidation within 30 days after return to the permanent official station for local travel (60 days after return to the Philippines for foreign); no additional cash advance to anyone with an unsettled previous cash advance; travel cash advances are exempt from the fidelity-bonding requirement; failure to liquidate triggers an auditor demand letter (after 2 months for AOs within the station, 3 months outside) and, 30 days after an unanswered demand, withholding of salary/any money due the officer; excess cash advance is refunded and evidenced by an official receipt.
  WHY: These deadlines and blocks are the workflow skeleton of the module: due-date computation, escalation states, the accountant's 'no unliquidated CA' certification, and refund-OR capture all come straight from this circular.
  SOURCE: https://rgao.upm.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/COA-C97-002-Cash-Advances-2-months-cash-flow.pdf
- PRACTICE: Approval chain: under EO 77, official local travel is approved by the head of agency or duly authorized representative; local travel of 30 days or more requires approval by the Department Secretary (or equivalent). DOH implements EO 77 through Department Order 2019-0225 (2019-07-10, amended by DO 2019-0225-A), which vests approval in the 'Authorized Official' â€” the head of the office with legal authority to approve travel (e.g., the Bureau Director for BLHSD staff). Typical bureau flow: employee files TO request + IoT, Division Chief recommends, Bureau Director approves; Budget certifies availability of allotment (ORS), Chief Accountant certifies completeness/prior-CA liquidation on the DV, Cashier disburses; liquidation returns through Accounting and is post-audited by the resident COA auditor.
  WHY: The DOH reference tenant's routing must be modeled as a configurable approval matrix (role x travel duration x traveler rank), because delegation differs per office and DO amendments can rewire it without touching EO 77.
  SOURCE: https://ncroffice.doh.gov.ph/Content/Public/Downloads/do2019-0225.pdf
- PRACTICE: Deduct components the traveler did not actually bear: no DTE component may be claimed when lodging and/or meals are provided free by the government, host agency, or event sponsor (subtract 50% for lodging provided, 30% for meals provided). Transportation is reimbursed at actual fare (economy/ordinary class; no first class) from the official station to destination and back, and no transport claim is allowed when a government vehicle is used. For NGO/privately sponsored conventions and seminars, DBM NBC No. 596 (2025-01-20) layers additional participation rules on top of EO 77's DTE rules.
  WHY: Double payment (claiming DTE for sponsor-provided meals/lodging, or fare while riding a government vehicle) is among the most common COA disallowance findings for travel claims; per-day 'provided by host' flags make the deduction mechanical.
  SOURCE: https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2025/National-Budget-Circular/NBC-No.-596.pdf
- PRACTICE: Distinguish the two money paths and compute settlement server-side: (a) cash advance before travel, then liquidation (Liquidation Report netting CA against allowed expenses, producing either a refund with OR number/date or a payable difference), and (b) straight reimbursement after travel with no CA, paid via Disbursement Voucher with the same supporting documents. Agencies may impose internal deadlines stricter than COA's 30 days (e.g., UP Manila requires liquidation within 15 days of return), so the deadline must be tenant-configurable with 30 days as the legal ceiling.
  WHY: The refund/payable computation and its OR evidence are exactly what the auditor traces from the LR to the books; a stricter configurable internal deadline also gives agencies buffer to meet the hard COA deadline.
  SOURCE: https://rgao.upm.edu.ph/2021/01/11/liquidation/

## PITFALLS
- Mislabeling forms: under GAM 2015, Appendix 45 is the Itinerary of Travel and Appendix 44 is the Liquidation Report â€” the 'Appendix 44 IoT / Appendix 45 CTC' numbering is the obsolete pre-2015 NGAS scheme; shipping the wrong form numbers destroys credibility with accountants and auditors.
- Hardcoding PHP 2,200 as 'the' DTE: the rate is per destination cluster (1,500/1,800/2,200) and each travel day takes the rate of that day's destination, so multi-leg trips crossing clusters need per-day rates; rates also change by future EO, so they must be effective-dated config rows.
- Paying 100% DTE on the return day â€” the day of departure from the destination back to the official station earns only 50% (meals + incidentals); this single rule error inflates every claim and is trivially caught in post-audit.
- Accepting an affidavit/certification of loss in place of hotel bills and official receipts â€” COA Circular 2012-001 explicitly rejects it; the UI must not offer it as an attachment type that satisfies the lodging requirement.
- Allowing a new travel cash advance while a previous one is unliquidated: PD 1445 sec. 89 and COA 97-002 forbid it, and the Chief Accountant's certification presupposes the system actually checks; a warning instead of a hard block makes the certification false.
- Ignoring host-provided meals/lodging: paying full DTE when the seminar sponsor fed and housed the traveler is double compensation and a stock COA disallowance; every day needs 'lodging provided / meals provided' flags that mechanically strip 50%/30%.
- Counting the 30-day liquidation clock from the wrong anchor: it runs from the date of return to the permanent official station, not from travel start, claim filing, or check release.
- Date-boundary bugs from UTC storage: 'day of arrival', 'full day', and deadline dates are Asia/Manila calendar days; computing day types or the 30-day deadline on raw UTC timestamps shifts days for evening arrivals (Office-Connect already stores timestamptz UTC, so all day-type derivation must go through the Manila conversion in core/time.py).
- Treating the Certificate of Appearance as optional: it is a required liquidation document proving the traveler actually appeared at the destination office/event; claims without it (or a documented justified exception) get returned.
- Skipping deviation handling: when actual travel differs from the approved itinerary, COA requires a Revised/actual Itinerary of Travel plus justification, and the Certificate of Travel Completed must disclose the deviation â€” a system that only stores the original IoT cannot produce a compliant liquidation.
- Assuming the digital record replaces paper originals: COA post-audit still examines original receipts, tickets, and signed certifications; the system is the workflow and computation of record, but it must track custody/submission of physical originals rather than pretend scans suffice.
- Treating EO 77 as frozen: annual GAA general provisions, DBM circulars (e.g., NBC 596 for NGO-sponsored events), and possible future EOs adjust the regime; a rules engine without effective dating will silently compute against superseded rates.

## RECOMMENDATIONS
- Model the rate rules as data, not code: reimb_dte_clusters (cluster, rate, effective_from/to) seeded with EO 77 Annex A (I=1500, II=1800, III=2200) and reimb_region_clusters mapping PSGC region codes to clusters, both effective-dated and tenant-overridable â€” DOH offices in LGUs or future tenants under different regimes then need only new rows, never a migration of logic.
- Build a pure, server-side computation service (per the existing 'no client-side money math' rule) that takes the itinerary legs and returns a per-day breakdown: day type (arrival/full/departure/same-day), applicable cluster rate, component amounts (50/30/20 as numeric(12,2)), deductions for host-provided lodging/meals, and the 50-km/no-overnight gate â€” and persist that breakdown row-per-day so the printed IoT/LR and the audit trail show exactly what was computed and why.
- Implement the documentary checklist as a rules-driven state machine keyed to COA Circular 2023-004: required-document sets for (a) CA grant, (b) liquidation, (c) straight reimbursement, with conditional items derived from claim facts (overnight stay â†’ hotel OR required; deviation â†’ Revised IoT required; excess claim â†’ agency-head certification required; refund due â†’ OR number/date required). Block state transitions, not just submissions, until the set is satisfied, and version the checklist config since COA revises it (2012-001 â†’ 2023-004).
- Generate print-faithful PDFs of GAM Vol. II Appendix 45 (Itinerary of Travel), 44 (Liquidation Report), 46 (RER), 47 (Certificate of Travel Completed) and the Appendix 32 DV from structured data, storing form template versions in config; assign Travel Order numbers from the existing XX-YYYY-NNNN never-reused reference-number service.
- Encode the approval workflow as a configurable matrix per tenant/office: default DOH-bureau chain (traveler â†’ Division Chief recommend â†’ Bureau Director approve) with an automatic reroute to the Office of the Secretary lane when planned duration >= 30 days (EO 77), plus the fiscal certifications as distinct workflow roles (Budget Officer: allotment/ORS; Chief Accountant: completeness + no unliquidated CA; Cashier: disbursement). Obtain DOH DO 2019-0225 and DO 2019-0225-A as the authoritative delegation source for the reference tenant and load them as tenant config, since the public PDFs are scanned/inaccessible.
- Enforce cash-advance discipline in the domain layer: a partial unique/exclusion constraint or guarded query preventing a new travel CA for any employee with an open (unliquidated, past-due or not) travel CA; liquidation due date = Manila-calendar return date + configurable N days (default 30, ceiling 30, allow stricter like 15); Celery-driven reminder ladder (T-7, T-1, overdue) escalating to 'demand letter' and 'salary-withholding recommended' states at the COA 97-002 thresholds â€” every escalation event written to the hash-chained core_audit_logs.
- Capture the facts the validations need as first-class fields: permanent official station per employee (staff directory), destination place + region per leg, arrival/departure datetimes per leg (stored UTC, day-typed in Asia/Manila), overnight-stay attestation for within-50km travel, government-vehicle flag per leg (suppresses fare claim), per-day host-provided lodging/meals flags, and OR number/date for refunds.
- Handle the excess-claim path explicitly: an 'actual exceeds prescribed rate' sub-workflow requiring the uploaded head-of-agency necessity certification plus receipts, computing and capping the allowable excess, and flagging the claim for heightened review â€” never silently paying above Annex A rates.
- Record deviations structurally: keep the approved IoT immutable (soft-delete/versioning already standard in this codebase), require a Revised IoT version with a justification note when actuals differ, and have the generated CTC text disclose the deviation â€” the diff between IoT versions is gold for the audit chain.
- Ship a physical-document tracking state per attachment (uploaded scan â†’ original received by Accounting â†’ forwarded to COA) so the system complements rather than contradicts COA's paper post-audit, and keep a compliance watch task (documented in the module doc's delta register) to review each year's GAA general provisions and any new EO/DBM circular amending EO 77 rates before renewing the seeded config.
