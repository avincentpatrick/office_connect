# agent7
TOPIC: Compliance and data-protection gates for Office-Connect: RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act) obligations for a PH government office, COA/NAP records retention for travel claims, financial-system audit-trail expectations, and maker-checker role separation

## KEY PRACTICES
- PRACTICE: Conduct a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) BEFORE implementing any new system that processes personal data. NPC Advisory No. 2017-03 makes this an expectation for government agencies; for agencies processing records of 1,000+ individuals (including personnel), NPC recommends assessing controls against ISO/IEC 27002 and ISO/IEC 29151 as the minimum benchmark.
  WHY: Office-Connect processes employee personal data (staff directory, travel claims, survey responses) for a DOH bureau, so a PIA is a hard prerequisite â€” 'before implementing' means before real employee data enters any environment, including UAT seeded with production data. The PIA also produces the data-flow map and risk register that drive the control framework and DPS registration.
  SOURCE: https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/NPC_AdvisoryNo.2017-03.pdf
- PRACTICE: Register the Data Protection Officer and each Data Processing System with the NPC via the NPC Registration System (NPCRS) under NPC Circular No. 2022-04. Mandatory triggers: 250+ employees, sensitive personal information of 1,000+ individuals, processing likely to pose risk to data subjects, or â€” in ALL instances â€” any system involving automated decision-making or profiling. Registration certificates are valid 1 year; renewal is due 30 days before expiry; amendments within 10/30 days.
  WHY: DOH easily exceeds both thresholds, so Office-Connect must be registered as a DPS (or added to the bureau's existing registration) before production. If any workflow rule auto-approves/auto-routes claims based on employee data, registration becomes unconditionally mandatory regardless of thresholds. Government DPOs also carry rank requirements (e.g., Assistant Secretary-level for a department).
  SOURCE: https://www.alburolaw.com/registration-of-data-processing-system-and-designation-of-data-protection-officer-mandated-by-npc-circular-no-2022-04/
- PRACTICE: Base employee-data processing on legal mandate / public function (RA 10173 Sec. 12(c) legal obligation, Sec. 12(e) public authority function; Sec. 13(b) processing required by existing laws and regulations with adequate safeguards) â€” NOT on consent. Issue privacy notices and record acknowledgment instead of collecting consent for mandated processing; reserve consent for genuinely optional features.
  WHY: Processing travel reimbursements, payroll-adjacent data, and personnel records is required by law for a government office, so consent is the wrong lawful basis: it must be freely given and withdrawable, and an employee cannot meaningfully refuse. Using legal mandate keeps processing valid when an employee objects, but requires the safeguards ('adequate safeguards' clause) to be demonstrable â€” which the PIA and control framework provide.
  SOURCE: https://privacy.gov.ph/data-privacy-act/
- PRACTICE: Implement the security measures in NPC Circular No. 2023-06 (Security of Personal Data in the Government and Private Sector; in force 30 March 2024, repealing Circular 16-01, with a 12-month transition to 30 March 2025): encryption of personal data at rest and in transit (AES-256 recommended, carried over from 16-01), multi-factor authentication for personnel accessing personal data online, defined/controlled access rights, a documented control framework, a Privacy Management Program, periodic personnel training, and a business continuity plan.
  WHY: This is the current binding security baseline for a government PIC. Office-Connect's on-prem Windows Server deployment gets no cloud-provider inheritance â€” encryption at rest, MFA, access logging, backup, and BCP must all be delivered by the platform and the office's own operations.
  SOURCE: https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/18922-implemented-national-privacy-commission-npc-circular-2023-06-on-security-of-personal-data-in-the-government-and-private-sector
- PRACTICE: Treat RA 10173 Chapter VII (Secs. 22â€“24) as a distinct government-sector overlay: the head of agency is personally responsible for security of sensitive personal information; SPI must be secured with the most appropriate ICT-industry standard as recommended by NPC; and off-site/online access to SPI in government systems is restricted (security clearance, encryption, and a cap of 1,000 records for off-site access).
  WHY: Office-Connect will hold sensitive personal information (health-related data in a DOH context, government IDs on reimbursement attachments). The 1,000-record off-site cap and clearance requirement directly constrain how bulk exports, remote access, and reporting downloads may be designed.
  SOURCE: https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2012/08/15/republic-act-no-10173/
- PRACTICE: Build breach-notification readiness to NPC Circular 16-03: notify the NPC AND affected data subjects within 72 hours of knowledge or reasonable belief of a notifiable breach; full report within 5 days (extendable only by NPC); no delay allowed when 100+ data subjects are affected or SPI disclosure would harm subjects; filings go through the NPC's Data Breach Notification Management System (DBNMS). NPC Advisory 2026-02 (May 2026) clarified that filing postponement/exemption requests does NOT suspend the 5-day report and only written, express NPC approval counts.
  WHY: 72 hours is unachievable without pre-built detection (the audit chain helps), an incident runbook, a breach register, and templated notifications. The 2026 advisory removes any wiggle room: the clock runs regardless of pending requests, so the system must make 'what was accessed, whose data, when' answerable within hours.
  SOURCE: https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/sgd-npc-circular-16-03-personal-data-breach-management.pdf
- PRACTICE: Apply the NAP General Records Disposition Schedule (GRDS, 2023 series, circulated with NAP General Circular No. 5) to financial records: paid disbursement vouchers and supporting documents (which is what a travel reimbursement claim becomes) carry a 10-year retention period, counted from final settlement, and may not be disposed while under audit or involved in any case. Under RA 9470 (National Archives Act), NO government record may be destroyed without prior written authority of the NAP Executive Director, via an approved Records Disposition Schedule â€” violations carry P500kâ€“P1M fines and 5â€“15 years imprisonment.
  WHY: Travel claims, itineraries, certificates of travel, and attached receipts are COA-auditable financial records. The platform must retain complete, reconstructable claim records (including attachments and workflow history) for at least 10 years after settlement, must support audit/litigation holds, and must never auto-purge â€” disposal is a human, NAP-authorized process the system can only track.
  SOURCE: https://nationalarchives.gov.ph/downloads/nap-general-circular-1-2-and-grds/
- PRACTICE: Reconcile DPA retention limits with archival law: the DPA/IRR require personal data be retained only as long as necessary for the declared purpose or as required by law â€” and law-mandated retention (GRDS 10-year, COA audit needs) is itself a valid basis. Declare retention periods per record class in the privacy notice and PIA, and dispose (with NAP authority) once both the business purpose and the legal retention period lapse.
  WHY: The two regimes pull in opposite directions (privacy says minimize, archives law says keep 10 years). The defensible position is a documented per-table retention class: DPA compliance is shown by declaring the legal basis for the 10-year hold, and by actually disposing after it ends rather than keeping data forever.
  SOURCE: https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/IRR_RA-10173-as-amended.pdf
- PRACTICE: Provide COA auditors direct, read-only access to the electronic records and audit trail. COA Resolution No. 2020-034 affirms auditors' authority to access agencies' information/communication systems, electronic documents, and source documents in the conduct of audit; computerized systems are expected to keep an audit trail of who changed what and to reproduce records in consistent, printable form.
  WHY: The hash-chained core_audit_logs is the right foundation, but COA expectations add: a dedicated auditor role with read access to claims + attachments + full history, a chain-verification report proving log integrity, and exportable/printable renditions of vouchers and trails â€” the audit trail must be usable BY a reviewer, not just tamper-evident.
  SOURCE: https://legal.uplb.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/2020-COA-Resolution-No.-2020-034-Authority-to-COA-Auditor-to-Access-ICS-EDM-and-Source-Documents_Conduct-of-Audit.pdf
- PRACTICE: Enforce the government disbursement certification chain (COA Circular No. 92-389 and the Government Accounting Manual DV format) as workflow states: Box A â€” certification by the responsible officer with direct supervision/knowledge of the transaction; Box B â€” certification by the Accountant (funds availability, completeness of supporting documents, journalization); Box C â€” approval by the Head of Agency or duly authorized official. Each box is a distinct person in a distinct role.
  WHY: A travel reimbursement ultimately rides on a Disbursement Voucher, so the in-system workflow (employee files â†’ supervisor certifies â†’ accounting certifies â†’ approving authority approves) must mirror the paper chain COA will audit against, with the signatory identity, role, and timestamp captured per step in the audit log.
  SOURCE: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68682
- PRACTICE: Apply NGICS (DBM National Guidelines on Internal Control Systems, 2008) segregation-of-duties: the person who initiates/prepares a transaction must be different from the persons who certify, record, and approve it; custody, authorization, and recording functions are separated; and controls must be designed into systems, not bolted on.
  WHY: Maker-checker is the internal-control norm COA examines in government financial processes. In Office-Connect this must be enforced structurally (RBAC + workflow + DB constraints), not by convention â€” e.g., an admin must not be able to file and approve the same claim, even with multiple roles.
  SOURCE: https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/SPIB-ICIA/3.%20NGICS.pdf
- PRACTICE: Run a standing privacy compliance program, not one-time checks: registered DPO involvement in design reviews, an annually renewed NPCRS registration, a records-of-processing register per module, periodic staff privacy training, and an annual security incident/breach summary report to NPC.
  WHY: NPC circulars (2022-04, 2023-06, 16-03) all create recurring obligations â€” annual registration renewal, annual incident reporting, ongoing training. A multi-module platform that keeps adding data processing systems (Reimb, DMWIS, CSS-IS) needs each new module to re-trigger PIA + registration-amendment + notice updates as a repeatable gate.
  SOURCE: https://privacy.gov.ph/npc-issues-circulars-to-strengthen-personal-data-protection-in-ph/

## PITFALLS
- Using consent as the lawful basis for employee reimbursement data: consent from an employee to their employer is presumptively not freely given and is withdrawable, which would make mandated processing unlawful the moment someone withdraws. Use legal mandate/public function and say so in the privacy notice.
- Treating soft deletes as retention compliance. Soft-deleted claims are still government records: they count toward the 10-year GRDS retention, must remain producible to COA, and conversely must eventually be disposable through a NAP-authorized process. Soft delete solves 'no hard DELETE', not records disposition.
- Auto-purging or cascading cleanup jobs of any financial record: destroying public records without NAP written authority is a criminal offense under RA 9470 (up to P1M fine, 15 years imprisonment, perpetual disqualification). Any 'cleanup' Celery task touching claim data is a landmine.
- Running UAT or demos with real employee data before the PIA is done â€” NPC Advisory 2017-03 expects the PIA before the system processes personal data, and test environments are the classic leak vector.
- Forgetting that automated decision-making triggers unconditional NPC registration: an innocuous 'auto-approve claims under X pesos' or auto-routing rule makes DPS registration mandatory in all instances under Circular 2022-04.
- Missing the annual NPCRS renewal (certificate valid 1 year, renewal due 30 days before expiry) or failing to amend the registration when DMWIS/CSS-IS modules add new processing systems.
- Letting role-stacking defeat maker-checker: a user granted both 'supervisor' and 'accountant' roles, or an admin override path, silently collapses Box A/B/C into one person. The check must be per-transaction identity comparison (created_by != certified_by != approved_by), not just role labels.
- Building an audit trail that is tamper-evident but not reviewer-usable: COA auditors need read access, human-readable history per claim, printable/exportable renditions, and a way to verify the hash chain â€” a chain only verifiable by developers fails the audit-trail purpose.
- Logging personal data into the audit chain immutably: because hash-chained logs cannot be redacted, writing full names, ID numbers, or medical details into log payloads creates un-erasable personal data. Log entity references and field names, not sensitive values.
- Designing unlimited exports/reports of employee data: RA 10173 Sec. 22 caps off-site access to sensitive personal information in government systems at 1,000 records with security clearance â€” bulk CSV downloads of the staff directory or claims violate this by design.
- Assuming on-prem Windows Server inherits security: no cloud KMS, no managed TLS, no managed backups. Encryption at rest (AES-256), MFA, backup/BCP, and physical access control are all NPC Circular 2023-06 obligations that Office-Connect's deployment docs must own explicitly.
- Waiting for a breach to design breach response: the 72-hour NPC/data-subject notification and unconditional 5-day full report (reaffirmed by NPC Advisory 2026-02 â€” pending requests do not pause the clock) require pre-built incident detection, a breach register, and notification templates.
- Multi-tenant leakage: a configurable multi-tenant platform where tenant scoping is only in application code (not enforced in queries/RLS and tested) turns one bug into a reportable cross-agency personal data breach.

## RECOMMENDATIONS
- Add a 'Compliance gates' section to the phase plan with explicit sequencing: (1) PIA + privacy notice + data-flow map completed BEFORE any real employee data enters any environment (gate on the first pilot, not on production); (2) NPC DPS registration/amendment + NPC Circular 2023-06 security checklist + breach runbook BEFORE production go-live; (3) NAP-aligned retention/disposal procedure documented before the first claim reaches final settlement (the 10-year clock starts there, so this can land in a later phase, but the schema support cannot).
- Create docs/compliance/ now with four living artifacts: pia-reimbursement.md (per-module PIA, re-run for DMWIS and CSS-IS), processing-register.md (records of processing activities per module), breach-runbook.md (72h/5-day timeline, DBNMS steps, contact list, notification templates), and retention-schedule.md (per-table retention class mapped to GRDS items). Wire these into the session-end checklist the same way module docs are.
- Bake retention metadata into the schema in the next migration wave: a retention_class column (or a core_retention_classes lookup) on claim/document tables, a legal_hold boolean + hold_reason (COA audit / pending case blocks disposal), and a retention_starts_at set at final settlement, not at creation. Never implement automated purge â€” implement a 'disposal eligibility report' that a human takes to NAP; actual disposal is a supervised, NAP-authorized batch with its own audit entries.
- Model the reimbursement workflow as the COA DV certification chain: distinct states for supervisor certification (Box A), accounting certification of funds/completeness (Box B), and approving-authority approval (Box C), each requiring a different authenticated user. Enforce maker-checker at three layers: workflow guard (state transition requires role), DB CHECK/trigger (created_by, box_a_by, box_b_by, box_c_by pairwise distinct), and a lint-style test that no seeded role combination can hold two boxes for the same claim. Any auto-approval rule ships behind a feature flag defaulting OFF, and enabling it triggers the NPC registration review (automated decision-making = mandatory registration).
- Extend the hash-chained core_audit_logs for reviewer use, not just integrity: a read-only 'auditor' role (COA resident auditor / internal audit) scoped to claims + attachments + full event history; a per-claim human-readable timeline view; a chain-verification endpoint producing a signed/printable verification report; and export of any claim as a complete paper-equivalent dossier (DV + supporting docs + approval history). Keep personal-data values OUT of immutable log payloads â€” log entity IDs and changed-field names, resolve display values at read time.
- Adopt legal mandate (RA 10173 Secs. 12(c)/(e), 13(b)) as the declared lawful basis for all mandated employee processing; implement a privacy-notice acknowledgment table (notice version, user, timestamp) rather than a consent table for those flows. Add a consent mechanism only for genuinely optional processing (e.g., CSS-IS respondent emails, optional profile fields), with per-purpose granularity and withdrawal.
- Map NPC Circular 2023-06 controls to concrete backlog items for the on-prem Windows Server target: TLS termination with managed cert renewal, PostgreSQL encryption at rest (full-volume BitLocker at minimum, pgcrypto for high-sensitivity columns like government ID numbers), MFA for all staff-facing logins (mandatory for admin/HR/accounting roles), account lockout + session timeout, tested backup/restore with offsite copy, and a documented business continuity plan. Record each as done/pending in a compliance checklist doc â€” the head of agency is personally accountable under Sec. 22, so the office will need this evidence.
- Design exports and reporting around the Sec. 22 off-site rule: paginate/cap bulk exports of sensitive personal information (hard ceiling well under 1,000 records per export), require an elevated approval (DPO or head-of-office role) for larger extracts, watermark and log every export (who, what filter, row count, when) in the audit chain, and prefer in-system dashboards over downloadable files for HR/claims data.
- Build breach readiness features in the foundation phase: a core_security_incidents table (append-only, like audit logs) capturing detection, scope, affected data subjects, and timeline; Celery-scheduled escalation reminders at T+24h/T+48h against the 72-hour deadline once an incident is opened; and a query pack that answers 'which records/users were touched by account X between T1 and T2' fast â€” that query is what makes the 72-hour notification content possible.
- Make multi-tenancy a privacy control, not just a product feature: enforce tenant scoping at the query layer (and consider PostgreSQL row-level security as defense-in-depth given the oc_app role pattern already in place), add cross-tenant isolation tests to CI, and document the isolation model in each PIA â€” for NPC purposes, each tenant agency is a separate PIC and a cross-tenant read is a reportable breach.
- Coordinate with the DOH/BLHSD DPO early (Phase 0/1, not at go-live): confirm whether Office-Connect registers as a new DPS under the department's existing NPCRS registration or needs its own entry, get the PIA template/sign-off route they use, and put the annual registration renewal and annual NPC security-incident report on a recurring operational calendar handed to the office, since these outlive the development engagement.
- Update docs/standards/database-standards.md with two clarifications this research surfaced: (a) soft delete is a data-integrity mechanism, NOT records disposition â€” GRDS retention and NAP disposal rules govern actual record lifecycle; (b) audit-log payload policy: no sensitive personal data values in immutable logs. Both are cheap to codify now and expensive to retrofit.
