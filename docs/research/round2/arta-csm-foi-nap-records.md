# agent8
TOPIC: Philippine government standards for Office-Connect reference features (round 2): ARTA harmonized Client Satisfaction Measurement (MC 2022-05 as amended by MC 2023-05), FOI under EO No. 2 s. 2016, and NAP records management under RA 9470 for the Document Tracking IS

## KEY PRACTICES
- PRACTICE: ARTA CSM instrument is FIXED, not designable: the harmonized questionnaire (Annex A of ARTA MC 2022-05, revised by MC 2023-05) = 3 Citizen's Charter awareness questions (CC1-CC3) + 9 Service Quality Dimension statements (SQD0-SQD8) on a 5-point agreement Likert scale (Strongly Agree=5, Agree=4, Neither Agree nor Disagree=3, Disagree=2, Strongly Disagree=1) plus an N/A option, with demographic fields: client type (Citizen / Business / Government-employee-or-another-agency), date, sex, age, region of residence, and service availed.
  WHY: The css_ module must render this exact instrument (with allowed Filipino/local-language localization per MC 2023-05) or the agency's CSM data is non-compliant and unusable in the annual report to ARTA.
  SOURCE: https://arta.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/MC-2022-05-GUIDELINES-ON-THE-IMPLEMENTATION-OF-THE-HARMONIZED-CLIENT-SATISFACTION-MEASUREMENT.pdf
- PRACTICE: Exact SQD wording (verbatim): SQD0 'I am satisfied with the service that I availed.'; SQD1 (Responsiveness) 'I spent a reasonable amount of time for my transaction.'; SQD2 (Reliability) 'The office followed the transaction's requirements and steps based on the information provided.'; SQD3 (Access & Facilities) 'The steps (including payment) I needed to do for my transaction were easy and simple.'; SQD4 (Communication) 'I easily found information about my transaction from the office or its website.'; SQD5 (Costs) 'I paid a reasonable amount of fees for my transaction.'; SQD6 (Integrity) 'I feel the office was fair to everyone, or "walang palakasan", during my transaction.'; SQD7 (Assurance) 'I was treated courteously by the staff, and (if asked for help) the staff was helpful.'; SQD8 (Outcome) 'I got what I needed from the government office, or (if denied) denial of request was sufficiently explained to me.'
  WHY: Question text must be stored verbatim per version; SQD5 commonly gets N/A for free services (e.g., internal services like reimbursement), which affects the score denominator.
  SOURCE: https://www.tsu.edu.ph/media/mbxiy0i3/client-satisfaction-measurement-form.pdf
- PRACTICE: CC questions with skip logic: CC1 'Which of the following best describes your awareness of a CC?' options: (1) I know what a CC is and I saw this office's CC; (2) I know what a CC is but I did NOT see this office's CC; (3) I learned of the CC only when I saw this office's CC; (4) I do not know what a CC is and I did not see one in this office. CC2 (answer only if CC1=1-3) 'Would you say that the CC of this office was...?' options: Easy to see / Somewhat easy to see / Difficult to see / Not visible at all / N/A. CC3 (only if CC1=1-3) 'How much did the CC help you in your transaction?' options: Helped very much / Somewhat helped / Did not help / N/A.
  WHY: The survey engine must enforce conditional display (CC2/CC3 only when CC1 is 1-3) and the CSM report must tabulate percentage per option â€” there is no pass/fail score for CC, only distribution reporting.
  SOURCE: https://www.tsu.edu.ph/media/radfcyxq/client-satisfaction-measurement-form.pdf
- PRACTICE: Exact CSM scoring formula: per-SQD score and Overall score = (count of 'Strongly Agree' + count of 'Agree') / (total responses to that question EXCLUDING N/A) x 100. The Overall score is computed across SQD1-SQD8 responses (SQD0 is reported separately as overall satisfaction). Interpretation bands per MC 2023-05: below 60.0% = Poor; 60.0-79.9% = Fair; 80.0-89.9% = Satisfactory; 90.0-94.9% = Very Satisfactory; 95.0-100% = Outstanding. ARTA expects agencies to reach at least 80% (Satisfactory).
  WHY: This is the formula the app must compute server-side per service, per office, per period, and for the consolidated annual figure; including N/A in the denominator or counting 'Neither Agree nor Disagree' in the numerator produces a wrong (non-compliant) score.
  SOURCE: https://www.arta.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/MC-2023-05_Amendment-to-CSM-1-1.pdf
- PRACTICE: CSM covers BOTH external services (citizens/businesses/other agencies) and internal services (employees availing internal processes such as disbursement/reimbursement); results must be disaggregated by service and by external vs internal in the CSM Report, with response counts vs total transaction volume per service and the sampling method (census for low-volume services; otherwise ARTA-prescribed minimum sample sizes with stated confidence level and margin of error).
  WHY: Office-Connect's own modules (travel reimbursement, room booking, document requests) are internal services under the Citizen's Charter â€” each completed transaction is a CSM survey trigger, and per-service tallies are mandatory report content.
  SOURCE: https://boi.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/BOI_CSMR_2023_04292024.pdf
- PRACTICE: CSM Report (CSMR) must follow the Annex B outline: Title page (agency, 'Client Satisfaction Measurement Report', year, edition); Table of Contents; I. Overview; II. Scope (period covered, geographic/office coverage, list of services surveyed with number of responses and total transactions); III. Methodology (survey modes physical/digital, sampling, confidence level and margin of error); IV. Data and Interpretation (respondent demographics, CC1-CC3 results, SQD results overall and per service, free-form comments/complaints summary); V. Results of the Agency Action Plan reported in the previous year; VI. Continuous Agency Improvement Plan for the following year; Annexes (questionnaire used, consolidated CSM results per service/office).
  WHY: This is the exact document the unified-reports module should auto-generate; sections V and VI mean the app must persist last year's action plan and track its status year-over-year.
  SOURCE: https://arta.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/ARTA-MC-2022-05_Annex-B_CSM-Report-Outline.pdf
- PRACTICE: CSMR filing deadline: the RA 11032 IRR / original rule set the submission 'on or before the last working day of January of every year'; ARTA MC 2022-01 extended and harmonized this, and the operative practice (confirmed for FY2024 reports) is submission to ARTA on or before April 30 of the following year, via ARTA's online submission channel announced by annual advisory (historically a Google-form/portal link and the artacsm@arta.gov.ph mailbox), with the PDF report also posted on the agency website/Transparency Seal.
  WHY: The app needs a compliance-calendar entry with a configurable deadline (default April 30) and an export pipeline, because ARTA announces the exact channel yearly and non-submission affects the agency's ARTA report card and PBB eligibility.
  SOURCE: https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/04/21/news/national/submit-csm-report-govt-agencies-lgus-told/2095528
- PRACTICE: FOI (EO No. 2, s. 2016, executive branch): every agency must publish a People's FOI Manual (EO 2 required it within 120 calendar days of effectivity) naming its FOI Receiving Officer (FRO) and FOI Decision Maker (FDM), the request flow, standard request form, fee rules (no filing fee; only reasonable reproduction/copying costs), and remedies; requests arrive via paper/standard channel or the eFOI portal (foi.gov.ph), and each request gets a tracked reference number.
  WHY: The Document Tracking IS should model FOI requests as a first-class correspondence type with FRO/FDM roles, because the manual's published workflow is what auditors and the FOI-PMO check against.
  SOURCE: https://pco.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/PCO-Peoples-FOI-Manual-022023.pdf
- PRACTICE: FOI statutory clock (corrects the project doc's '3/15/30'): respond within 15 WORKING days from receipt of a complete request; ONE extension of not more than 20 additional working days is allowed for voluminous records, inter-agency referral, or similar difficulty, and the requester must be notified in writing BEFORE the original 15 days lapse (max 35 working days total); failure to reply on time is a deemed denial. Denials must be in writing, citing the specific exception from the DOJ/OSG-approved inventory of exceptions. Appeal: within 15 CALENDAR days from notice of denial (or lapse), to the next higher authority/agency FOI appeals body, decided within 30 WORKING days; thereafter judicial remedies (mandamus).
  WHY: The SLA engine must be working-day aware (Philippine holiday calendar), enforce the single-extension rule with mandatory notice, auto-flag deemed denial, and open a 15-calendar-day appeal window â€” the '3-day' tier in the source doc belongs to RA 11032 transaction classification (3/7/20 working days for simple/complex/highly technical), a separate regime the tracking IS should model independently.
  SOURCE: https://www.pdic.gov.ph/files/foi/Annex_A-EO_2_FOI.pdf
- PRACTICE: FOI reporting to the FOI-PMO (PCO): agencies maintain a Standard FOI Registry â€” a log of ALL requests (standard + eFOI) with reference number, date received, requester type, information requested, action taken (granted/denied/referred), date of response, days lapsed, and fees â€” submitted QUARTERLY together with the Agency Information Inventory (per FOI Memorandum Circulars No. 1 and 5, s. 2017), plus an annual FOI summary report; registries/reports are publicly disclosed on the agency FOI page.
  WHY: The app must be able to export the registry in the FOI-PMO template per quarter and compute 'days lapsed' automatically; this is the recurring compliance artifact, not just the individual request handling.
  SOURCE: https://www.foi.gov.ph/resources/policies/
- PRACTICE: RA 9470 (National Archives of the Philippines Act of 2007) obligations for every government office: designate a records officer/custodian; run a records management program; conduct a records INVENTORY and keep a registry of holdings (by record series, location, inclusive dates, volume in cubic meters); prepare a Records Disposition Schedule (agency RDS for substantive records; the NAP General Records Disposition Schedule (GRDS) for common/facilitative records) with NAP approval; NEVER dispose of or destroy any public record without prior WRITTEN authority from the NAP Executive Director; transfer records of enduring/archival value (and records over 30 years old) to NAP. Unauthorized destruction carries criminal penalties.
  WHY: This is the legal backbone for the Document Tracking IS and controlled document repository: every stored document should carry a record-series classification with retention metadata, and the platform's no-hard-delete rule is not just good practice â€” hard deletion of public records is a crime.
  SOURCE: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/19575
- PRACTICE: NAP prescribed forms the module should generate: NAP Form No. 1 â€” Records Inventory and Appraisal (descriptive listing per record series: location, inclusive dates, volume); NAP Form No. 2 â€” Records Disposition Schedule (per record series: retention period in office area, in storage area, and disposition/preservation-or-destruction provision); NAP Form No. 3 â€” Request for Authority to Dispose of Records (list of valueless records due for disposal, submitted to NAP for appraisal by a Records Management Analyst and approval by the Executive Director, followed by witnessed disposal and a certificate/report of disposal).
  WHY: These are the exact filing artifacts of the records lifecycle; if the app tracks series, retention clocks, and disposal batches, it can print Forms 1-3 instead of staff rebuilding them in Excel.
  SOURCE: https://www.tesda.gov.ph/Uploads/File/prescribed%20forms/quality00/NAP-Form-2-rds.pdf
- PRACTICE: Current NAP issuance baseline: NAP General Circulars No. 1 and 2, s. 2009 (public records management rules and disposal procedure) and NAP General Circular No. 5, s. 2023 â€” 'Guidelines on the Disposal of Valueless Records in Government Agencies' â€” which also promulgates the NEW General Records Disposition Schedule (GRDS series of 2023) of retention periods for records common to all agencies (correspondence, personnel, financial, procurement, etc.).
  WHY: Retention-period lookup data seeded into the app must come from the 2023 GRDS, not the 2009 one; the circular also defines the current disposal workflow the app's disposition module must follow.
  SOURCE: https://nationalarchives.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/NAP_General_Circular_No_5.pdf
- PRACTICE: Document registry discipline the Tracking IS should mirror from Philippine records-office practice: separate incoming and outgoing correspondence registers with per-year sequential control numbers, capture of date/time received, source/addressee, subject, attached document count, action officer, routing/endorsement chain, and release/dispatch details; every movement logged (matches the RA 9470 registry duty and COA/ISO audit expectations).
  WHY: This registry structure is what auditors and NAP inspections look for, and it maps cleanly onto the platform's existing XX-YYYY-NNNN reference-number and hash-chained audit-log standards.

## PITFALLS
- Implementing the FOI clock as '3/15/30 days' as stated in the project reference doc â€” the real structure is 15 working days + one extension of max 20 working days (notice required before lapse), 15 CALENDAR days to appeal, 30 WORKING days to decide the appeal; the 3-day figure belongs to RA 11032's 3/7/20 working-day transaction tiers, a different regime.
- Computing the CSM score with N/A responses in the denominator or counting 'Neither Agree nor Disagree' as satisfied â€” the compliant formula is strictly (Strongly Agree + Agree) / (responses excluding N/A) x 100.
- Hardcoding the CSMR deadline as 'last working day of January' from the RA 11032 IRR / MC 2022-05 text â€” ARTA MC 2022-01 harmonized/extended it and the operative deadline is April 30 of the following year, announced (with the submission channel) by annual ARTA advisory, so both must be configurable.
- Building the CSM survey for external clients only â€” internal services (reimbursement, HR, supply requests) are in scope and must be surveyed and reported separately from external services.
- Shipping the 2022 questionnaire without versioning â€” MC 2023-05 revised questions, interpretation bands, and localization rules; the questionnaire, formula, and bands must be versioned records with effective dates, and further amendments should be config, not code.
- Skipping CC2/CC3 conditional logic (they must appear only when CC1 = options 1-3) or omitting the N/A choices â€” this corrupts both distribution tables and score denominators.
- Counting FOI/CSM deadlines in calendar days uniformly â€” the app needs a Philippine working-day calendar (national + local holidays, suspensions) because FOI processing and appeal-decision periods run on working days while the appeal filing window runs on calendar days.
- Allowing any hard delete or ad-hoc purge of tracked documents â€” disposal of public records without written NAP authority (Form 3 approval by the NAP Executive Director) is criminally punishable under RA 9470; deletion must be modeled as a gated 'disposition' state change with the authority document attached.
- Seeding retention periods from the 2009 GRDS or from memory â€” NAP General Circular No. 5, s. 2023 promulgated a new GRDS; retention values must be imported from it and kept as editable lookup data.
- Forgetting the year-over-year dependency in the CSMR: sections V (results of last year's action plan) and VI (next year's improvement plan) require persisting action plans across reporting years, which a naive one-shot report generator cannot produce.
- Treating the FOI Registry as an internal log only â€” it is a quarterly deliverable to the FOI-PMO (with the Agency Information Inventory) and publicly disclosed, so requester personal data handling must anticipate publication (masking) under the Data Privacy Act.

- Store all official templates (harmonized CSM questionnaire per version, FOI request/denial/extension notice templates from the People's FOI Manual, NAP Forms 1-3) in the QMS controlled-document module with version history and download permissions, so form updates are content changes, not deployments.
