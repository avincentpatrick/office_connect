- PRACTICE: ISO QMS certification is validated for the Performance-Based Bonus: the AO 25 Inter-Agency Task Force issues 'Guidelines on the Compliance with, and Validation of, ISO QMS Certification or Recertification' (Annex 3 of the PBB guidelines); certification bodies accredited by DTI-Philippine Accreditation Bureau (or an IAF member) are required/preferred. - PRACTICE: ISO 9001:2015 clause 6.1 (actions to address risks and opportunities): when planning the QMS the organization must determine risks AND opportunities arising from its context (4.1) and interested-party requirements (4.2), plan actions to address them, integrate the actions into QMS processes, and evaluate the effectiveness of those actions; actions must be proportionate to the potential impact on conformity of products/services. ## PITFALLS
- Hardcoding one risk matrix: agencies use 3Ã—3, 4Ã—4 or 5Ã—5 with locally defined criteria. Make likelihood/impact scales and band thresholds a versioned, per-tenant configuration â€” and NEVER silently rescore historical entries when the matrix changes (version-stamp each assessment with the criteria version used).
- Forgetting opportunities: ISO 9001 6.1 covers risks AND opportunities; PH agencies name the artifact 'Risk and Opportunity Registry'. A risk-only schema will fail the first surveillance audit question about opportunities (responses: exploit/enhance, not just mitigate).
- Treating the registry as a one-time compliance document. Auditors check review dates, treatment effectiveness evaluation, and residual-risk re-scoring. Without enforced review cadence (annual minimum, or per-cycle) and an 'effectiveness of actions' field, the registry is dead paper.
- Management Review minutes that don't track prior actions: 9.3.2(a) â€” status of actions from previous reviews â€” is the first required input. If the system doesn't carry the matrix of agreements forward with per-item status (open/ongoing/closed), every review is a compliance gap.
- Conflating three related but distinct registers: the QMS Risk Registry (ISO 9001, owned by process owners/QMR), the internal-audit risk assessment (PGIAM, owned by the Internal Audit Service, used for audit planning), and enterprise risk management (GCG-mandated for GOCCs only). Build the QMS registry first; expose exports the others can consume, but don't merge ownership.
- Allowing edits to signed/approved records: MR minutes and approved registry versions are 'retained documented information'. Once approved they must be immutable (soft-delete/append-only + hash-chained audit log â€” aligns with existing standing rules 5 and 6).
- Inventing a 'national mandatory risk register form' â€” none exists. GQMC manuals are guidance; each agency's own QMS procedure (with its own document code, revision number and effectivity date) defines the binding format. The module must therefore let the tenant set form codes/revision numbers via the controlled-document module rather than emit a fictitious national form number.
- Computing risk scores client-side. Score = likelihood Ã— impact and band mapping must be server-computed (this also violates the project's existing no-client-side-math rule if done in the UI).
- Missing the certification/PBB linkage: incomplete MR records or a stale risk registry can cost the agency its ISO certificate, which is validated under the AO 25 PBB guidelines â€” treat completeness validation (all 9.3.2 inputs present before an MR can be marked 'conducted') as a hard rule, not a nice-to-have.
- Storing only scanned PDFs of minutes/registries. Keep structured data (typed inputs, decisions, action items) so input packs, trend charts and next-cycle carry-forwards can be auto-assembled; attach the signed scan as evidence, don't make it the record.
- Ignoring quality-objectives monitoring: DOH practice monitors quality objectives per office against targets; MR input (c) requires 'extent to which quality objectives have been met'. Without a quality-objectives target/actual tracker somewhere (this module or Reports), the MR input pack has a permanent hole.
