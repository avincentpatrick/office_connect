## PITFALLS
- Confusing the two card systems: the Stock Card/Property Card (Supply unit, quantities) and the Supplies Ledger Card/PPE Ledger Card (Accounting unit, quantities + money) are separate legal records kept by different offices â€” merging them into one table destroys the reconciliation control COA audits against.
- Using PAR for items below â‚±50,000 or ICS for items at/above â‚±50,000 â€” the custody form follows the capitalization threshold, and mixing them misclassifies assets in the financial statements and puts items on the wrong physical-count report (RPCPPE vs RPCSP).
- Treating the â‚±50,000 test as per-transaction instead of per-unit: individual unit cost governs; bulk purchases of many sub-â‚±50k items remain semi-expendable even if the invoice total is large (COA 2022-004 guidance).
- Hard-deleting or silently dropping lost/unserviceable property from records â€” items must remain on the books and in the accountable officer's balance until an approved IIRUP (disposal) or COA relief from accountability (loss) authorizes derecognition; PD 1445 Sec 73's 30-day relief application window cannot be met if the loss event isn't timestamped.
- Missing the fixed report calendar: RPCI and RPCSP due Jan 31 and Jul 31 (semestral), RPCPPE due Jan 31 (annual), PIF to GSIS/COA due Apr 30, PO copies to COA within 5 working days of issuance â€” these are the dates auditors check first.
- Allowing client-side or ad-hoc costing: moving average unit cost must be recomputed server-side at every receipt and applied to every issue; letting users type unit costs on RIS issues breaks the perpetual inventory valuation.
- Reusing form serial numbers or keeping one global sequence: PR/PO/RIS/IAR/ICS/PAR numbers are serially assigned per fund cluster per year and are never reused â€” voided numbers must remain visible as voided.
- Forgetting semi-expendable is expensed upon issuance, not on purchase: on receipt it sits in a Semi-expendable Property inventory account; the expense and the ICS accountability arise when issued to the end-user, and returns need a receipt-of-return document reversing custody.
- Overstocking: procurement rules limit routine supply purchases to reasonable short-term requirements and COA treats inventory grossly exceeding normal needs (commonly benchmarked at ~3 months' consumption) as excessive expenditure â€” the module should surface months-of-stock-on-hand.
- Ignoring the signature/witness matrix: physical-count reports need Inventory Committee certification + Head of Agency approval + COA verification; disposal proceedings must be witnessed by COA; an IAR needs both inspector and acceptor. Workflows with a single approver will not satisfy audit.
- Building disposal as a single 'dispose' action instead of a pipeline (declare unserviceable â†’ IIRUP â†’ appraisal â†’ mode selection per COA 89-296 â†’ COA witnessing â†’ proceeds/receipt recording â†’ derecognition), each stage with its own document and date.
- Relying on pre-2022 references that still say â‚±15,000: the reference specs in `references/` or older GAM commentary may carry the superseded threshold; the module should store the threshold as a config value with an effectivity date, not a hard-coded constant.
