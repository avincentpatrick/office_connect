TOPIC: Controlled Document Management for Philippine Government QMS (ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.5 + EO 605/GQMC conventions + RA 9470 records disposition) â€” what the Office-Connect Controlled Document Management module must encode

## KEY PRACTICES
- PRACTICE: ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.5.2 â€” every controlled document, on creation and on every update, must carry identification and description (title, unique document number/code, date, author/owner), a defined format and media, and traceable review-and-approval for suitability and adequacy (it must be clear WHO reviewed/approved and WHEN).
  WHY: This is the certifiable minimum: an approved document without a code, revision, or traceable approver is an automatic audit nonconformity. The module's document record must make code, revision, and the Prepared-by/Reviewed-by/Approved-by trail mandatory fields, not optional metadata.
  SOURCE: https://preteshbiswas.com/2019/05/11/iso-90012015-clause-7-5-documented-information/
- PRACTICE: ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.5.3 â€” documented information must be (a) available where and when needed and (b) adequately protected from loss of confidentiality, improper use, and loss of integrity; the system must address distribution/access/retrieval/use, storage/preservation (incl. legibility/backups), change control (version control), retention and disposition, identification and control of documents of external origin, protection of records from unintended alteration, and prevention of unintended use of obsolete documents (mark them if retained).
  WHY: Clause 7.5.3 is the functional spec of the module: access control, watermarking, version supersession, obsolete-marking, external-document register, and retention rules each map to a 7.5.3 bullet that an ISO auditor will test line by line.
  SOURCE: https://preteshbiswas.com/2019/05/11/iso-90012015-clause-7-5-documented-information/
- PRACTICE: Executive Order No. 605, s. 2007 institutionalizes ISO 9001 QMS in all Philippine government agencies under the Government Quality Management Program, overseen by the Government Quality Management Committee (GQMC, chaired by DBM), with the Government Quality Management Systems Standards (GQMSS) as the prescribed guidance; AO 161 s. 2006 is the precursor. GQMC Memorandum Circulars (2018-1, 2019-1, 2020-1, 2021-1) make validated ISO 9001:2015 QMS certification/recertification a Performance-Based Bonus (PBB) requirement.
  WHY: This is why a 40-person DOH bureau runs a QMS at all: certification is tied to the agency's PBB. The module should therefore mirror certifiable ISO conventions exactly, because its outputs (master lists, DCR trails, obsolete stamps) are what external certification auditors and GQMC validators inspect.
  SOURCE: https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2007/02/23/executive-order-no-605-s-2007/
- PRACTICE: Philippine agency document hierarchy is consistently 4 levels: Quality Manual (Level 1) â†’ Quality/System Procedures (Level 2) â†’ Work Instructions / Operations Manuals / SOPs (Level 3) â†’ Forms & Templates (Level 4). DOH facilities use typed codes, e.g. NCH-QM-2022 (Quality Manual), NCH-QP-<Service/Dept>-<Section/Unit>-<control no.> (procedures), plus WI, SOP, TP, OM, QO types; other agencies: DOF-QMS-QM-001, TESDA-QM, COA-QMSM-01, DILG forms like FM-SP-RO1-01A-01.
  WHY: The module needs a configurable document-code generator of the pattern <AGENCY/OFFICE>-<TYPE>-<UNIT>-<SEQ>, a document-type taxonomy (QM/QP/WI/SOP/OM/FM...), and a hierarchy field â€” these are the de facto conventions every PH agency QMS uses and what the reference tenant (DOH BLHSD) will expect.
  SOURCE: https://nch.doh.gov.ph/images/ISO/9-_Control_of_documented_info.pdf
- PRACTICE: Standard PH-government document-control block on every controlled document: Document Code, Revision No. (integer starting at Rev. 0, incremented on each approved change), Effectivity Date, Page X of Y, and a Prepared by / Reviewed by / Approved by signatory block. Originals are stamped MASTER COPY; issued paper copies are stamped CONTROLLED COPY with a copy number tied to a distribution list; anything else is UNCONTROLLED.
  WHY: These exact fields must appear on every rendered/downloaded document and in the register UI; Rev. No. + Effectivity Date is how users verify currency, and copy-numbered controlled distribution is how retrieval of superseded copies is proven during audits.
  SOURCE: https://nch.doh.gov.ph/images/ISO/9-_Control_of_documented_info.pdf
- PRACTICE: COA's QMS manual carries the canonical PH wording for electronic repositories: 'THIS DOCUMENT WHEN PRINTED is an UNCONTROLLED COPY' â€” users must 'ENSURE that the printed copy being used is the current version by checking the effectivity date of the CONTROLLED COPY in the FILE SERVER/WEBSITE'. The electronic copy in the official repository is the controlled copy; every print/download is uncontrolled by default.
  WHY: This is the exact model Office-Connect should implement: the in-app version is the controlled copy; every PDF download gets an automatic 'UNCONTROLLED WHEN PRINTED/DOWNLOADED' watermark plus download date and downloader identity, unless issued through the controlled-copy distribution workflow.
  SOURCE: https://www.coa.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/transparency/COA_QMS_Manual.pdf
- PRACTICE: Changes go through a Document Change Request (DCR). DILG RO1's DCR form (FM-SP-RO1-01A-01) has request types Creation / Change (revision) / Deletion (obsolescence), with fields: date of request, document title and code, proposed changes, current and new revision numbers, effectivity date, reason for request, and Prepared/Reviewed/Approved signatories; for new documents the signatories come from a Document Responsibility Matrix, for revisions they are the same authorities as on the original document.
  WHY: The DCR is the single workflow entity of the module: no controlled document may be created, revised, or retired except through an approved DCR, and the routing (originator â†’ process owner review â†’ QMR endorsement â†’ head-of-office approval â†’ Document Controller registration) is the state machine to encode.
  SOURCE: https://region1.dilg.gov.ph/images/Transparency/QMSFINAL/FINAL%20SP%20for%20print.pdf
- PRACTICE: Role separation is standardized: the Process Owner drafts and reviews content; the QMR (Quality Management Representative) endorses/reviews for QMS conformity; top management (agency/office head, e.g. Regional Director) approves; the Document Controller assigns codes, maintains the Master List of Internal Documents and Master List of External Documents plus revision histories and distribution lists, releases controlled copies, and retrieves/stamps obsolete copies.
  WHY: These four roles map directly to the module's RBAC: author/edit rights (process owner), endorse rights (QMR), approve rights (head), and registry/distribution rights (document controller) â€” and the Master Lists must be system-generated reports, not hand-maintained spreadsheets.
  SOURCE: https://region1.dilg.gov.ph/images/Transparency/QMSFINAL/FINAL%20SP%20for%20print.pdf
- PRACTICE: Documents vs. records: controlled DOCUMENTS (manuals, procedures, WIs, blank forms/templates) are revisable and version-controlled; RECORDS (accomplished forms â€” evidence of conformity) are never revised, must be protected from alteration, and are retained/disposed per a retention schedule. A blank form is a document; the filled form is a record.
  WHY: The module must model these as two different object types with different lifecycles: documents get revisions and effectivity dates; records get retention periods, read-only protection, and disposition workflows. Mixing them is both an ISO nonconformity and a data-model trap.
  SOURCE: https://iso-docs.com/blogs/iso-9001-standard/iso-9001-clause-7-5-documented-information
- PRACTICE: Records retention/disposition in PH government is governed by RA 9470 (National Archives of the Philippines Act of 2007): no government office may dispose of or destroy public records without prior written authority of the NAP Executive Director; each agency must maintain a Records Disposition Schedule (RDS, filed on NAP Form No. 2) consistent with NAP's General Records Disposition Schedule (GRDS, updated via NAP General Circulars, e.g. GC No. 5 on disposal of valueless records); disposal is requested via NAP Form No. 3 (Request for Authority to Dispose of Records) listing records series, period covered, retention period, location and volume.
  WHY: The module's 'disposition' feature can never be a delete button: it must track retention periods per record series from the agency RDS/GRDS, flag eligibility for disposal, and generate the NAP Form 3 request package â€” with actual destruction happening only after NAP authority is recorded. This aligns exactly with the project's no-hard-delete rule.
  SOURCE: https://nationalarchives.gov.ph/downloads/nap-forms-2/
- PRACTICE: External documents (documents of external origin used in the QMS â€” laws, DOH Administrative Orders/Department Orders/Department Memoranda/Department Circulars, COA/CSC/DBM circulars, ISO standards) must be identified in a separate Master List of External Documents and controlled for currency. DOH issuance types are formally defined (AO = policies/standards implementing health laws; DO = instructions to particular persons; DC = internal administration) and numbered YYYY-NNNN (e.g., AO 2021-0008, DC 2024-0129, DM 2023-0320).
  WHY: For the BLHSD tenant, the repository of 'issuances/guidelines/memos' the user described IS the external-document register plus internal issuance library â€” the module should parse/validate the YYYY-NNNN convention, record issuing authority and date of adoption, and track superseding issuances.
  SOURCE: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/11/42559
- PRACTICE: Document control is the most common ISO 9001 audit nonconformity category worldwide. Recurring findings: obsolete or superseded documents still in use at workstations, unapproved/unregistered versions in circulation, uncontrolled printed copies, missing approval signatures, records that cannot be retrieved on request, version mismatches between locations, and master lists that disagree with documents actually in use.
  WHY: Each classic finding is a feature requirement in reverse: single-source current-version view, auto-supersession, watermarked prints, mandatory signatory capture, full-text/code search with fast retrieval, and a master list generated live from the register so it can never disagree with reality.
  SOURCE: https://www.9001simplified.com/learn/document-control-iso-9001.php
- PRACTICE: Approved documents carry an Effectivity Date distinct from the approval date; agencies publish/enforce the document only from its effectivity date, keep a Revision History page (rev no., date, description of change, reason) inside each document, and conduct periodic review of documents even when unchanged (typically annually to every 3 years), re-confirming or re-issuing them.
  WHY: The module needs scheduled effectivity (approved-but-not-yet-effective state), an auto-maintained revision-history table rendered into each document, and next-review-date reminders â€” auditors ask 'when was this last reviewed?' even for stable documents.
  SOURCE: https://psc.gov.ph/psc_site/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/DocumentedInformationProcedure_PAWIM_UncontrolledCopy_1.pdf

## PITFALLS
- Treating documents and records as one entity type â€” blank templates (revisable, effectivity-dated) and accomplished forms (immutable evidence with retention periods) have opposite lifecycles; one table with a 'type' flag and shared update logic will produce ISO nonconformities.
- Allowing in-place edits to an approved/effective version. Any content change must force a DCR and a new revision row; the old revision is superseded, never mutated (this also matches the project's hash-chained audit rule).
- Hard-deleting obsolete documents or records. RA 9470 makes unauthorized destruction of public records unlawful; obsolete documents must be retained, marked OBSOLETE, and access-restricted â€” which the project's soft-delete rule already supports, but the UI must also visibly stamp them, not just hide them.
- Serving clean, unwatermarked PDFs on download. Every ad-hoc download must render the UNCONTROLLED-copy watermark plus document code, rev no., effectivity date, page X of Y; otherwise printed copies circulate as apparent controlled copies â€” the #1 real-world audit finding.
- Maintaining the Master List of Documents as an editable page. It must be a live report generated from the register; a manually maintained list will drift and auditors diff it against reality.
- Confusing approval date with effectivity date, or letting effectivity precede approval. The state machine needs Draft â†’ For Review â†’ Endorsed (QMR) â†’ Approved â†’ Effective (on effectivity date) â†’ Superseded/Obsolete, with the Effective transition time-triggered.
- Global revision counters or reused document codes. Revision numbers are per-document starting at 0; document codes are never reused after obsolescence (mirror the project's 'ref numbers never reused' rule).
- Forgetting external documents. A register that only holds internally authored procedures fails 7.5.3.2(f); DOH AOs/DMs/DCs, COA and CSC issuances the bureau relies on must be listed, dated, and tracked for supersession.
- Access control that fails open on download permissions. The user explicitly wants download restriction; default must be view-only in-app, with download/print as an explicit per-role, per-document-type grant, and every download written to the audit log (who, when, which revision).
- Skipping retrieval tracking for issued controlled copies. If numbered controlled copies (physical or exported) are issued, the system must track their return/retrieval when a new revision becomes effective â€” auditors ask where copy #3 of the superseded procedure went.
- Ignoring the periodic-review clock. Documents with no changes still need review-by dates; without reminders the bureau will accumulate procedures 'last reviewed 2019' â€” a guaranteed surveillance-audit finding.
- Building the DCR as a free-form comment thread instead of a typed form. It needs the DILG-style fields (type: creation/revision/obsolescence; affected code; current+proposed rev; proposed changes; reason; signatories) so the DCR itself becomes the auditable record of change control.

## RECOMMENDATIONS
- Model two aggregates: `controlled documents` (with child `document revisions`) and `records series` (retention metadata linking to accomplished records elsewhere in Office-Connect). Per project DB standards this suggests e.g. docs_documents / docs_document_revisions / docs_change_requests / docs_external_documents / docs_distribution_copies / docs_records_series â€” plural, prefixed, soft-deleted, ownership-columned.
- Document entity fields: immutable document_code (configurable generator: <OFFICE>-<TYPE>-<UNIT>-<SEQ>, e.g. BLHSD-QP-LHS-001), document_type (QM/QP/WI/SOP/OM/FM/TPL â€” tenant-configurable lookup), hierarchy level 1â€“4, process_owner (org-unit + user), classification/access level, next_review_date, status. Revision entity: revision_no (int, starts 0), change_description, reason, approval trail, approved_at, effectivity_date, file reference, status (draft/approved/effective/superseded/obsolete).
- Encode the DCR as the only mutation path: types CREATION, REVISION, OBSOLESCENCE (mirror DILG FM-SP-RO1-01A-01 fields). Routing state machine: Originator â†’ Process Owner review â†’ QMR endorsement â†’ Approver (office head; configurable per Document Responsibility Matrix) â†’ Document Controller registration (assigns code/rev, sets effectivity, publishes). Persist every transition into the hash-chained core_audit_logs.
- Enforce effectivity logic server-side: effectivity_date >= approval date; revision becomes the single 'current' version only at effectivity (Celery beat job flips Approvedâ†’Effective and currentâ†’Superseded atomically); exactly one effective revision per document at any time (DB partial unique index).
- Render the control block into every generated PDF: header/footer with Document Code, Rev. No., Effectivity Date, Page X of Y, Prepared/Reviewed/Approved names+dates, plus an auto-built Revision History page. All rendering server-side (consistent with the no-client-side-computation stance).
- Watermark every ad-hoc download/print 'UNCONTROLLED COPY WHEN PRINTED OR DOWNLOADED â€” verify current revision in Office-Connect' with downloader name + timestamp (COA model: the in-app copy is the controlled copy). - Make download/print a distinct permission from view, grantable per document-type and per document (the user's 'download permissions' requirement); default deny. Log every view and download (user, revision, timestamp) â€” this doubles as the 7.5.3 'access, retrieval and use' evidence and feeds internal-audit reports.
- Auto-generate the Master List of Internal Documents (code, title, type, rev, effectivity, process owner, location) and Master List of External Documents as live, exportable reports â€” these are the two artifacts certification auditors always request; wire them into the unified Reports/'Government Outputs' screen.
- Build the external-document register for laws and issuances: issuing authority, issuance type (DOH AO/DO/DM/DC, COA/CSC/DBM circulars, ISO standards), number in YYYY-NNNN format with validation, date issued, date adopted, custodian, superseded_by link. - Obsolete handling: on supersession or obsolescence-DCR approval, stamp the stored file OBSOLETE (server-rendered overlay), restrict access to Document Controller + auditors, keep it forever (soft-delete philosophy), and show it only under an explicit 'archived/obsolete' filter â€” never in default search results.
